Glossary — the signals{% if show_windows %}, the window, the filter{% endif %} and the terms

The signals — EV SQ TS CM

How many rows the Published Store holds for this Fixture, in four kinds. They are signals, not gates: none of them decides whether a Fixture can be drafted. A Fixture with all four missing still drafts — the prompt says what is absent in words rather than failing, and the Narrative comes out thinner. A Coverage-light Competition (ADR 0014) yields a timeline-only report, which is still worth writing, so nothing is ever withheld from this board for being thin.

EV Events The timeline — one row per time-stamped moment of play: goals, cards, substitutions, VAR, each with its minute, Team and Player. Distinct from the per-match totals on a Squad Entry, which say how many but not when. Shootout kicks are not Events (ADR 0013) — the shootout is a Fixture-level fact.
SQ Squad Entries The matchday squads — one row per named player (~22 a side), tagged started, came_on or unused_sub. Feeds the lineups and the standout players. A Season without player Coverage carries none at all, which is the Season's shape, not missing data.
TS Team Match Stats Possession, shots, xG, corners, fouls and cards — the Team-level line. Two rows is complete, one per Team, so 2 is the only healthy value. The prompt needs both sides to tabulate them: with one row it writes (team match stats not available), exactly as it does with none.
CM Commentary Lines ESPN's narration of the match, reached through the optional and hand-supplied Fixture link. Given to the model for colour and specific detail, never to quote. Absent on most Fixtures until you supply an ESPN game id — so CM is the honest answer to “have I linked ESPN for this one yet?”. With none, the commentary section is left out of the prompt entirely rather than flagged.

The window — 7 14 30 90 days

How far back the board looks, counted from each Fixture's kickoff, newest first. The default is {{ default_days }} days — long enough to catch a weekend you did not get to, short enough that the page stays a work list rather than an archive.

The window is a display choice and nothing more. It is not part of what a Drafting Candidate is: a Fixture that falls off the window is still Final, still has a Publication, still has no Match Post — and is still reachable directly at /candidate/<fixture id>, which ignores the window entirely. Any number works, not just the four links: ?days=365 is a season. Refresh Candidates uses whichever window you are on.

The Competition filter

Narrows the board to one Competition — its range is every Competition a Publication covers, so one that is out of season stays selectable and tells you it is empty rather than going missing. The count beside each is how many Finals it has in the current window, so the numbers move when the window does.

Like the window, it is a display choice: it changes what you are looking at and nothing about what is draftable. Refresh Candidates ignores it and always refreshes every Publication's Competition — the frontier is not per-Competition work you would want to half-do.

The terms

Drafting Candidate
A Fixture ready to be written about: it is Final, its Competition has a Publication, and its Match Post is either absent or still a draft. Those are exactly the two conditions the pipeline refuses on, and no more — data completeness is never part of it. Not a queue with an order: it is a set, and which one to write is an editorial choice.
Final
A Fixture played to completion — FT, AET or PEN. Only a Final has per-fixture data to collect, which is why nothing else appears on the board.
Publication
Our editorial decision to cover a Competition, with the language and display timezone that decides which system prompt the model gets and how each kickoff is printed. A Competition without one never reaches this board. Its own published gate governs the public site alone — a Publication can be drafted against long before anything about it is public.
Match Post
The blog's unit of work: exactly one per Fixture, identified by that Fixture's id. Status draft or published. The drafter only ever writes draft. draft can be overwritten by running again — that is what a draft is for. published was edited by hand and moved by a human; it stops being a Candidate and shows here struck through, because “have I written this one?” is a question answered badly by omission.

These are the definitions in CONTEXT.md; the Desk is described in docs/adr/0034-the-desk.md.
